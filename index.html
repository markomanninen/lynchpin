Lynchpin in four dimensions

This is the Lynchpin of Terrence Howard, six regular pentagons that share one corner at the centre of a regular tetrahedron. Each pentagon is meant to lie between two of the four white rays with two of its sides on the rays. In three dimensions two rays stand 109.47° apart and the corner of a pentagon is 108°, so each pentagon leaves 1.47° of the angle between its rays uncovered and its sides miss the rays. Fold the rays 7.76° into a fourth dimension and every side lies on its ray. The closed figure is the set of edges and pentagons at one vertex of the 120-cell, a regular four-dimensional polytope. The screen shows the projection of the folded figure into three dimensions, which draws the closed pentagons slightly widened.

Rotate in 4D

A rotation in four dimensions turns two axes into each other and leaves the other two in place. Each slider turns one visible axis towards the fourth axis w, and the picture is the three-dimensional projection of the turned figure. Shadow drops the w coordinate. Perspective drops it too and first scales every point, so that points with a smaller w are drawn larger.
